A1 Research 2025 – 26

Diversity, Maximized

My research: robust, efficient algorithms that pick the most varied subset of a huge dataset, even when the data contains outliers.

Imagine millions of items (documents, products, images) and room to show only ten. Which ten? You want them as different from each other as possible, so together they cover the whole range. That's diversity maximization.

The catch is that real data is noisy. A few strange points sit far away from everything else, and the classic algorithms happily pick them because they look “very different”. With my advisors I designed algorithms that set those outliers aside and still scale to large inputs.

With
Andrea Pietracaprina & Geppino Pucci, University of Padova
Status

    Track A1 · Liner insert

    Choosing well, with outliers

    1. Set aside the outliers. The z most isolated points (red) are ignored. They look “different”, but they're just noise.
    2. Start at the edge. Pick the point farthest from the centre of the remaining data.
    3. Go farthest-first. Keep adding the point farthest from everything chosen so far, until you have k.

    The dashed circles never overlap. Their radius is half the smallest distance between two chosen points, and that distance is what we want to maximise.

    This is a simplified teaching version of the idea. The algorithms in the paper are designed for millions of points and come with provable guarantees.
